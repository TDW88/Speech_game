Islands (sound pairs)

Each island practices one pair of sounds. Turn islands on as the speech therapist moves to new sounds. The K vs T pictures are from this week’s homework sheet. The other islands use my own word picks, matched to the goals in the evaluation, so check them with the therapist before turning them on.

Your voice

Tap the red button and say the word once, clearly. It stops by itself and plays back. Until a word has your recording, the iPad voice reads it.

Other voice lines

Record what the game says, too. Say only the words shown. Names are recorded on their own, and the game slots them in where you see “…”. A line uses your voice once every piece of it is recorded. Until then, the iPad voice reads the whole line.

This button exports every recording: words, game lines and names. Recordings marked “this iPad only” are lost if Safari’s website data is cleared. Export them, add the voices folder from the zip to the game’s GitHub repo, and they’ll play on every device.

Progress

How to play along

  • Hide & Seek is the pointing game from the homework. If he taps the wrong picture, the critter peeks out from the right one and your word plays again. There’s no buzzer and no score.
  • In the Café, he names the order card. Tap the word you actually heard, even if it’s the wrong one. When he says “tea” for “cookie,” the critter brings tea, and that mix-up teaches him that sounds change meaning.
  • After a mix-up, your model plays and he gets one more try. If it’s still hard, the game moves on, as the therapist asked.
  • Use Skip if he didn’t really try (distracted, silly voice). Skipped words aren’t counted.
  • Practice (on the map) is flashcards for one island’s sound at the start, middle or end of words. Tap Said it right or Not yet. After “Not yet” your model plays and he gets one more try. Ten cards help one of that island’s critters grow.
  • Short and often beats long: one or two trips a day. Stop while he still wants more.
  • If he’s stuck on K, ask the therapist which cue she uses. Common ones are a little “cough” sound in the back of the throat or keeping the tongue tip down. Use her words so the cues match.
